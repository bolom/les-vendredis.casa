The cabin isn't in a clearing. It's in the forest. The Montravail forest covers the heights of Sainte-Luce, between Route de Zaire and Morne Gommier. It's a humid tropical forest at a modest altitude — we're 150 metres above the sea, not in the Amazon canopy. But it has its own density, its own life.

## What you hear

The rooster, obviously. Dimdim starts around five. But before him, there are the birds: the Guadeloupe woodpecker drumming, the Saint Lucia oriole whistling on a loop, the green-throated caribs buzzing around the bougainvillea. At night, the tree frogs take over. You also hear the wind in the bamboo — a background noise that never stops.

## What you see

Montravail is a mixed forest. Big trees — kapok, mahogany, gum trees — but also escaped fruit trees: mango, guava, avocado. Tree ferns colonise the wetter slopes. Lianas hang everywhere, some thick as an arm. Light arrives in patches, never direct.

In the rainy season, the ground gives off a smell of wet earth and decaying leaves. Fluorescent orange mushrooms grow on fallen trunks. Blue morphos cross the light gaps — a flash, then nothing.

## The tracks

This isn't an empty forest. Animal tracks are everywhere if you look: manicou scratching in dead leaves, bat droppings under the rocks, and sometimes, early morning, raccoon prints near the basin. Local hunters know this forest well — you'll cross their créole dogs from time to time, and Samsam answers them.

## What to know

Montravail forest isn't an official reserve. It belongs partly to private owners, partly to the commune. It's crossed by trails maintained by hikers and hunters, but it isn't set up as a park. No parking, no snack bar, no map at the trailhead.

To walk, start from the cabin. We'll show you the trails we know. Adapt your gear to the season — in the wet season, some sections get slippery. Respect the forest: it shelters us, and it shelters many other things.

## The best hour

Six in the morning. The light is low, the heat still bearable, and the forest belongs to the birds. If you're lucky, you'll hear a hummingbird before you see it.
